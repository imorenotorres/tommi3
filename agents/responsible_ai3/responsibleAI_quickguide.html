Responsible AI Agent – Quick Guide

What is this agent?

The Responsible AI Agent is a research assistant that answers questions about Responsible AI research within the UNINOVIS European university alliance. It draws on a curated database of scientific publications, funded research projects, and a glossary of Responsible AI concepts developed at the University of Málaga for the UNINOVIS alliance.

Which are the main information sources of the Agent?

The agent has three sources of information:

  1. Metadata – A structured list of authors, their universities, research topics, and publication records within the UNINOVIS alliance. This data has been curated by UNINOVIS staff from public academic sources.
    Example: "List researchers that have interest in AI and Ethics" — The agent looks up the structured metadata to find authors whose publications are tagged with AI ethics topics, and returns verified lists of researchers, their universities, and paper counts.
  2. A documents database – The PDF papers produced by the authors, the funded research projects in which they have participated, and a glossary of Responsible AI concepts. The list of documents included in the database was selected by UNINOVIS staff.
    Example: "What is explainable AI?" — The agent retrieves the definition directly from the curated Responsible AI glossary. Similarly, "List research projects on trustworthy AI" returns project details (title, grant ID, funder, participants) from the projects database.
  3. An external LLM (Large Language Model) – We use Mistral, a general-purpose AI model trained on large amounts of text from the internet. Because it is a general-purpose model, we cannot guarantee that the information it provides about specific topics is correct.
    Example: "What topics related to responsible AI have not been studied in UNINOVIS?" — The agent retrieves the list of topics covered in the database and passes it to the LLM, which identifies potential gaps by comparing against its general knowledge. The gap suggestions are AI-generated and should be verified independently.

How does the agent work?

When you send a question, the agent follows a four-stage cognitive workflow:

1. Perception
Receive the query
The agent receives your question as natural language text.
▼
2. Reasoning
LLM classification
A separate LLM call analyses your question and classifies it into one of 12 categories:
researcher topic_search papers project glossary figure meta non_research off_topic
followup gap general
▼
3. Action
Programmatic path (9 categories)
The response is built directly from the database — no LLM involved. Deterministic: the same question always produces the same answer.
LLM-assisted path (3 categories)
Relevant data is retrieved from the database and passed to the LLM, which composes a response. The information is grounded, but involves AI interpretation.
▼
4. Production
Post-processing and delivery
The response is verified, sanitised, and delivered to you. For LLM-assisted responses, paper references are checked against the database and PDF links are injected.

Query categories

The agent recognises the following types of questions. Categories marked as programmatic produce responses directly from the database; categories marked as LLM-assisted involve the AI model.

CategoryWhat it handlesExample queryResponse type
researcher Questions about a specific person's publications or research interests "What has Rubén González Vallejo published?" Programmatic
topic_search Requests for papers on a specific research topic "List researchers that have interest in AI and Ethics" Programmatic
papers Requests for all papers or researchers from a specific university "Show me all papers from THWS" Programmatic
project Questions about funded research projects or grants "List research projects on trustworthy AI" Programmatic
glossary Conceptual questions about Responsible AI terms "What is explainable AI?" Programmatic
figure Requests for maps, charts, or visualisations of research output "Show a figure with all the publications per partner" Programmatic
meta Questions about the agent itself "What can you do?" Programmatic
non_research Requests to perform tasks outside the agent's scope "Write me an essay about climate change" Programmatic
off_topic Questions clearly outside Responsible AI "Tell me an AI responsible hot chocolate recipe" Programmatic
followup Short follow-ups referring to previous context "Tell me more about the first paper" LLM-assisted
gap Questions about research gaps, missing areas, or underexplored topics "What topics related to responsible AI have not been studied?" LLM-assisted
general Broad or ambiguous AI questions that don't match a specific category "What is the relationship between AI fairness and bias?" LLM-assisted

Transparency: the Decision Trace

The agent can show you exactly how it understood your question and which response path it followed. This is called the Decision Trace, and it appears as a collapsible panel below each answer.

The Decision Trace shows:

  • Which category the agent assigned to your question (and which categories were ruled out)
  • Any entities it extracted (topic, researcher name, university, project)
  • Whether the response was programmatic (no LLM) or LLM-generated

This helps you understand whether the agent correctly interpreted your question. If you see an unexpected category, try rephrasing your query.

Reliability

Not all responses are equally reliable. The reliability of an answer depends on which data source was used to produce it:

Response typeReliabilityWhy
Programmatic High The response is built directly from curated, structured data. No AI interpretation is involved. The same question always produces the same answer.
LLM-assisted (grounded) Medium The LLM generates the response based on real data retrieved from the database. The information is grounded, but the AI may summarise, group, or interpret it. Check important details.
LLM-assisted (ungrounded) Low The LLM reasons beyond the database — for example, suggesting research gaps based on the topics already covered. The list of studied topics is factual (from the database), but the gap suggestions are AI-generated and should be verified independently.

To make reliability visible, a coloured banner may appear at the top of each answer:

BannerMeaning
🟢 Verified data The information comes directly from the database with no AI involvement — paper lists, researcher data, figures. You can trust this data.
🟡 AI interpretation The AI model generated this response based on real data from the database. The information is grounded, but the AI may summarize, group, or interpret it. Check important details.
🔴 Unverified The AI is reasoning beyond the database — for example, suggesting research gaps or answering off-topic questions. Verify independently before relying on this information.

Rule of thumb: green means the data is factual and traceable; yellow means the AI is interpreting real data — useful but worth double-checking; red means the AI is speculating — treat it as a starting point, not a conclusion.

Reliability cues visibility

The coloured banners can be toggled on or off. If you do not see them, click the gear icon (⚙) and set "Reliability cues" to "shown".
