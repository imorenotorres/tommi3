Responsible AI Agent – Quick Guide

What is this agent?

The Responsible AI Agent is a research assistant that answers questions about Responsible AI research within the UNINOVIS European university alliance. It draws on a curated database of scientific publications, researcher profiles, funded research projects, and a glossary of Responsible AI concepts contributed by the 8 UNINOVIS partner universities.

Which are the main information sources of the Agent?

The agent has three sources of information:

  1. Metadata – A structured list of authors, their universities, research topics, and publication records within the UNINOVIS alliance. This data has been curated by UNINOVIS staff from public academic sources.
  2. A documents database – The PDF papers produced by the authors, the funded research projects in which they have participated, and a glossary of Responsible AI concepts. The list of documents included in the database was selected by UNINOVIS staff.
  3. An external LLM (Large Language Model) – We use Mistral, a general-purpose AI model trained on large amounts of text from the internet. Because it is a general-purpose model, we cannot guarantee that the information it provides about specific topics is correct.

How does the agent select which data source to use?

Information across the three sources can overlap. The agent analyses your question and selects the most appropriate source. Depending on the type of question, a different workflow is followed:

Workflow 1 — Who, where, or how many?
e.g. "List researchers working on AI ethics", "Show publications per university", "How many papers in 2024?"
Your question ➔ Metadata ➔ Direct response (no LLM involved)
Workflow 2 — What does something mean? What does a paper say?
e.g. "What is explainable AI?", "What are the findings of paper X?", "Describe the project Y"
Your question ➔ Documents database (relevant passages retrieved) ➔ LLM composes the response
Workflow 3 — Beyond the database
e.g. "What research gaps exist?", "Tell me about AI regulation in China", "Write me a recipe"
Your question ➔ LLM responds from its own general knowledge (no curated data involved)

Up to what point is Responsible AI a reliable information source?

It depends on the data source used to answer your question.

  • When the agent draws from curated metadata, the information is highly reliable.
  • When it retrieves and summarises document content, the underlying data is real but the presentation involves AI interpretation.
  • When the answer relies on the external LLM, the information is unverified and should be treated with caution.

To make this visible, a coloured banner appears at the top of every answer:

BannerMeaning
🟢 Verified data The information comes directly from the database with no AI involvement — paper lists, researcher data, figures, glossary definitions. You can trust this data.
🟡 AI interpretation The AI model generated this response based on real data from the database. The information is grounded, but the AI may summarize, group, or interpret it. Check important details.
🔴 Unverified The AI is reasoning beyond the database — for example, suggesting research gaps or answering off-topic questions. Verify independently before relying on this information.

Rule of thumb: green means the data is factual and traceable; yellow means the AI is interpreting real data — useful but worth double-checking; red means the AI is speculating — treat it as a starting point, not a conclusion.
