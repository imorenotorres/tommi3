Agent-to-Human Interaction: Comparative Study

University of Málaga — Procedural Guidance vs. Content Transparency

Welcome & Informed Consent

Thank you for participating in this study. The experiment can be completed in 5–7 minutes.

Background: One key problem of today’s AI agents is that the information they produce is not always exact (they may even produce hallucinations). This places users in a difficult position: should I trust this response? All of it? Some parts? Which? The end goal of this study is to develop agents that do not raise such questions (or at least some of them).

In this experiment we test two alternative information-presentation approaches:

  • Content-based: adding specific facts that should allow you to better decide on the data reliability.
  • Guidance: providing scaled indications on the reliability of the data, so that you decide how to interpret it.

Data: Your email is collected only to prevent duplicate participation. A random participant ID is assigned for all analysis. Your email will not be shared or published. Data will be used for research purposes only.

Voluntary participation: You may withdraw at any time by closing this page. Incomplete responses will not be used.

Contact: For questions about this study, please contact the research team at the University of Málaga. Do not hesitate to ask if you need further clarification before starting the experiment.

Step 1: Structure of the Experiment

The experiment consists of the following steps:

  1. A brief background survey (3 questions).
  2. Read the baseline response of an AI agent to a predefined question.
  3. See the baseline response with a first type of added information, and answer 3 short questions.
  4. See the baseline response with a second type of added information, and answer the same 3 questions.
  5. Indicate your overall preference between the two approaches.
