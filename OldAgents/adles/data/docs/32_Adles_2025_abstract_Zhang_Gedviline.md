# *Abstract* 

# While traditional piano pedagogy is effective, its impact is constrained by the spatial and temporal limitations of in-person instruction, as well as teachers' finite capacity for detailed post-lesson analysis. This study proposes a hybrid teaching model that integrates high-fidelity distance instruction with an artificial intelligence (AI)-driven post-class analysis system, aiming to expand the boundaries of AI application in music education---transforming it from a real-time error-correction tool into an intelligent analytical assistant that supports teaching reflection and optimization.

# This study aims to design and validate a "Smart Class Review System". By analysing high-definition class recordings captured via technologies such as Yamaha's remote piano system, combined with multimodal AI analysis, the system automatically generates structured class summaries. The research seeks to explore its role in enhancing learning retention, supporting teaching reflection, and improving continuity between lessons.

# A design-based mixed-methods approach was employed, tracking eight intermediate piano learners and their teacher over one academic semester. Bi-weekly remote lessons were conducted using a high-fidelity audio/video and MIDI transmission system, with all sessions recorded and analysed through an AI pipeline integrating computer vision, automatic speech recognition, natural language processing, and music information retrieval. The system produced structured summaries containing technical key points, teaching highlights, and practice tasks, which were provided to both teachers and students. Data were collected through semi-structured interviews, utility questionnaires on the summaries, analysis of teacher annotations, and comparative assessments of learning outcomes.

# The core innovation of this study lies in redefining the role of AI from a "real-time correction tool" to a "post-class teaching analyst", pioneering the application of multimodal AI in teaching documentation and insight generation. Its practical significance includes: constructing a scalable framework for teaching knowledge repositories, alleviating teachers' note-taking burdens, and enhancing the quality of in-class interaction; providing students with precise and personalized review materials; and establishing a data-driven feedback loop for teaching optimization, thereby systematically improving the effectiveness and continuity of the remote music education ecosystem.

# This study has several limitations: the accuracy of AI-generated summaries depends on the performance of technologies such as speech recognition and natural language processing in artistic contexts; the small sample size and specific technical environment may affect the generalizability of the findings; and the subjective and complex nature of artistic instruction necessitates teacher review and correction of AI summaries, positioning them as supportive rather than authoritative teaching resources.

# Keywords: *AI music feedback*, *performing arts transformation*, *piano pedagogy digitalization*, *remote music education*.
