🔬 Molecule Viewer

Interactive 3D molecular visualization for biology and chemistry courses

What this tool does

Molecule Viewer lets students explore 3D molecular structures directly inside Moodle. Teachers curate a list of molecules per course by PDB ID; students can rotate, zoom and change the rendering style in their browser — no software to install.

  • Structures loaded from the RCSB Protein Data Bank (100,000+ structures)
  • Rendering styles: Cartoon, Stick, Sphere, Surface
  • Color schemes: Spectrum, Chain, Element (CPK)
  • No LLM — all rendering is client-side via 3Dmol.js

For teachers — how to add molecules

  1. Launch the tool from your Moodle course
  2. Click Add molecule in the sidebar
  3. Enter a PDB ID (4-letter code, e.g. 1CRN, 4HHB) and click Preview to verify
  4. Optionally set a display name, description and default rendering style
  5. Click Add to course — the molecule appears immediately for all students

Find PDB IDs at rcsb.org — search by molecule name, organism or function.

Tip: Use Cartoon for proteins and nucleic acids, Stick for small molecules and drugs, and Surface to visualise binding pockets.

Exploration features

  • Rendering styles: Cartoon (ribbons), Stick, Sphere (CPK) and Surface — switchable at any time from the controls bar.
  • Color schemes: Spectrum (rainbow by residue), Chain (one colour per chain) and Element (Jmol/CPK palette).
  • Ligands ✓ — when active, cofactors, drugs and small molecules are automatically highlighted with stick+sphere style in element colours, overlaid on the protein representation. Toggle it off to focus on the macromolecule alone. Particularly useful for enzyme–inhibitor complexes.
  • 📷 Exportar — downloads the current 3D view as a PNG image. Students can include the image in reports or presentations.
  • ⊞ Comparar — splits the viewer into two independent panels (A / B). Use the dropdowns to load different molecules side by side. Ideal for comparing conformational states (e.g. haemoglobin T vs R), or a protein before and after drug binding.
  • Search RCSB by name — inside the Add molecule modal, teachers can type a molecule name (e.g. “hemoglobin”) and browse results directly, without leaving the tool.
Tip: For enzyme–drug complexes such as 6LU7 (SARS-CoV-2 protease) or 1HSG (HIV protease with ritonavir), enable Ligandos to see the small-molecule inhibitor highlighted in the active site.
